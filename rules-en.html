Basic Rules of Baduk

Baduk has a very simple set of rules, but offers infinite strategic depth. On this page, you will learn the fundamental concepts to start playing.

The Board

The game is played on a board called a goban, which has 19×19 intersections (361 points total). Smaller boards also exist for beginners, such as 9×9 and 13×13.

Players use:

  • Black stones (Sente - the player who starts)
  • White stones (Gote - the second player)

Stones are placed at the intersections of the board, not in the squares. Once placed, a stone only moves if it is captured (removed from the board).

Objective of the Game

The objective is to control more territory than your opponent at the end of the game.

Territory is empty space surrounded by your stones. The more area you control, the higher your score. Victory goes to whoever has more territory at the end.

Liberties and Capture

A stone has liberties when there are empty points adjacent to it (above, below, left, or right). Stones of the same color that touch form a group, which shares its liberties.

When a group loses all its liberties, it is captured and removed from the board. This can happen when your opponent places a stone on the last empty point adjacent to the group.

Example: If a group has only one liberty remaining, it is said to be in atari (imminent danger). Your opponent's next move can capture it.

The Suicide Rule

A move is considered suicide when a placed stone creates no liberties for itself or its group. In other words, placing a stone that would result in its immediate capture is prohibited.

There is an exception: you can capture your opponent's stones while losing your own liberties, as long as your stone/group gains liberties from the capture.

The Ko Rule

The Ko rule prohibits you from immediately undoing your opponent's last move by capturing the same stone.

This rule prevents infinite loops where both players alternately capture and recapture the same stone. To capture again after a Ko, you must make at least one move elsewhere on the board.

Territory Counting

At the end of the game, the score is calculated by counting:

  • Empty points surrounded by each color's stones (territory)
  • Stones captured by the opponent (counted as points)

There are scoring variations (such as komi - a bonus given to white for having the second move), but the basic principle is: whoever controls more space wins.

End of Game

The game ends when both players pass consecutively (indicating there are no more profitable moves). At this point, the board is counted and the winner is determined.

A game of Go can last from a few minutes (on small boards or blitz) to several hours (in classic games between professionals).

Ready to Get Started?

Now that you know the basic rules, you're ready for your first game! Find an opponent, a board (or an online app), and start playing.
